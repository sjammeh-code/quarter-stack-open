How to open a bank Bookgi

A plain guide for first-timers. It explains what to prepare, what to bring, what happens at the bank, and what to do once your account is open.

Before you go

A little preparation saves you a second trip. Decide which bank suits you and which kind of account you need, then call or visit the branch to ask for its account opening checklist.

Choose a bank

Compare two or three banks. Look at monthly fees, the minimum balance, how close the branch is, and whether they offer a mobile app or a card.

Choose an account type

A savings account is for keeping money safe and may earn interest. A current account is for everyday payments and business use, with fewer limits but usually higher fees.

What to bring

Banks must confirm who you are before they open an account. This identity check is often called “know your customer”, or KYC, and it is required by law. Tick each item off as you collect it.

Check that your name matches. Your name should be spelled the same way on every document. A mismatch is the most common reason for delays.

At the office

Go early in the morning, when queues are shorter. Tell the customer service desk that you would like to open a new account, and they will guide you through three stages.

  1. Fill in the application form

    Write clearly, using your full legal name, and answer every question unless the form says it is optional.

    • You may be asked to name a next of kin, a person the bank can contact if something happens to you.
    • You may be asked for a sample signature, which the bank keeps to check future withdrawals.
    • Read the fees page before you sign.

  2. Wait for verification

    The bank copies your documents and checks your identity. Some branches finish this while you wait, and others take a few working days, so ask when you should expect to hear back.

  3. Make your first deposit

    Once you are approved, pay in your opening deposit at the counter and keep the receipt. You will be given your account number, so check that it matches your paperwork and write it down somewhere safe.

After you open the account

Depending on the bank, you will receive a debit card, a passbook or chequebook, and sign-up details for mobile or internet banking. A few habits keep your money safe from the start.

  • Choose a PIN that is not your birthday or phone number.
  • Never share your PIN or password, and never write them on the card.
  • Ask the staff to switch on SMS alerts so you see every transaction.
  • Make one small deposit or withdrawal to confirm everything works.

This page gives general guidance. Rules and documents vary by bank and by country, so confirm the details with your chosen branch before you go.
