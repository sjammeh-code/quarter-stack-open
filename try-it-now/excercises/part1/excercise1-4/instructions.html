How to run a petrol generator safely

This sheet is for anyone who starts the household or shop generator during a power cut. Read it every time you switch the generator on, because a mistake can cause a fire, an electric shock or fatal poisoning from exhaust fumes.

What you need

  • A generator in good working order, with its rating plate still readable
  • Fresh petrol in a closed, labelled fuel can, stored away from the generator
  • Engine oil, for topping up
  • A heavy-duty outdoor extension cord with no cuts or damage
  • A fire extinguisher or a bucket of dry sand within reach
  • A dry, level spot outside with a roof that is open on all sides
  • A torch, so you never need a candle near fuel
  • A battery-powered CO alarm in the rooms nearest the generator, if you have one

Steps

  1. Carry the generator outside. Put it at least 6 m from every door, window and vent, on dry, level ground under a roof that is open on all sides.
  2. With the engine off, check the fuel and oil levels. Top up if needed and wipe up any spills.
  3. Switch off and unplug every appliance, so the generator starts with nothing connected.
  4. Start the engine as shown in the manual: fuel tap on, choke set, then pull the cord or press the start button.
  5. Let it run for 2 min until the engine sounds steady. Plug in the extension cord only after that.
  6. Plug in appliances one at a time, biggest first, and keep the total below the output on the rating plate (shown in kVA or W). Plug into the extension cord only. Never plug the generator into a wall socket, because it sends power back down the line and can kill the people repairing it.
  7. To refuel, unplug the appliances and switch the engine off. Wait 15 min for it to cool before you open the fuel cap, then restart from step 4.
  8. To finish, unplug every appliance before you switch the engine off. Then close the fuel tap and let it cool completely before you move or store it.

If something goes wrong

If anyone feels dizzy, has a headache, feels sick or becomes weak while the generator is running, switch it off, get everyone into fresh air straight away and call for help, because CO has no smell and can kill quickly. If fuel catches fire, never throw water on it; use the extinguisher or smother it with sand, and if the fire spreads, leave and call the fire service.
